Perhaps no encounter in life is truly accidental—not even my encounter with the books that held my hand during lonely days and guided me through walls of silence toward brighter horizons of understanding. In those moments, books were more than a source of entertainment; they became a refuge, a doorway out of a world that sometimes felt smaller than my dreams. When the doors of formal education were closed to me, books became my unconventional classrooms, and every chapter I turned was a step away from the shadows of doubt and into the light of self-discovery. 

When I first read There Are No Coincidences, I did not realize that each page had the power to slowly reshape my way of thinking. It taught me to look for patterns in the chaos of my daily life, finding meaning even in the most restricted environments. Later, the Alchemist taught me that the greatest treasures are not always found in distant places; sometimes, they lie within ourselves. In Santiago's journey, I saw reflections of my own path—a journey where deserts are not always geographical landscapes, but can also be silence, limitations, and loneliness. That book forced me to confront the fear of the unknown and embrace my own personal legend, even when the road seemed impassable. 

Along the way, other books taught me that growth is the result of small but consistent efforts. From The Compound Effect, I learned that meaningful change begins with simple daily choices. I realized that my situation, however difficult, could not stop the momentum of my own intellectual expansion if I remained consistent. From Viktor Frankl, I learned that even in the most difficult circumstances, human beings can find a reason to keep going. His words became my anchor, reminding me that while I could not choose my circumstances, I could always choose my attitude toward them. These ideas were never just theories to me; they became tools for resilience that helped me navigate the psychological toll of being a student in a system that often denied my right to learn. 

During times when the world around me felt quieter than ever, the pages of books gave me a voice to listen to. Through reading The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People, I came to understand that life is not a finished project but an ongoing process of conscious choices. I learned that the responsibility for building my life rests on my own shoulders, even when circumstances are far from ideal. Books taught me that people can rise above limitations; that discipline is not merely a habit, but a quiet form of resistance against the chaos of life. Every small step and every daily effort contributes to the creation of a greater future. When I study Health Science today, I am guided by this same discipline, understanding that my ambition to contribute to public health is a direct result of these early lessons in self-management. 

Perhaps the most important lesson books have taught me can be summarized in this thought: everything unfolds in its own time. The difference between people is that some wait for life to come to them, while others rise and create their own paths. I have learned that there are no shortcuts to growth. A mature person is someone who embraces life with all its ups and downs, contradictions, and uncertainties—someone who understands that patience does not mean standing still, but moving forward consciously through hardship. This patience has been my strongest ally, allowing me to persist in my studies and my advocacy despite the many obstacles placed in my way. 

Books have shown me that identity is not fixed; it is shaped by the choices we make every day. Every idea that found its way into my mind brought me one step closer to understanding myself. I learned that true power lies in awareness, not in circumstances. I have realized that my struggle is not unique, but my response to it can be. By reading widely, I have connected with the wisdom of centuries, realizing that others have stood in my shoes and found ways to thrive. This realization has been deeply empowering. 

Today, when I reflect on my journey, I realize that books have helped shape the direction of my life. They have taught me that knowledge is not only for learning but also for building meaning, purpose, and a future that may once have seemed out of reach. I have transitioned from being a girl who feared the silence to a student who uses her voice to contribute to a better understanding of public health and education. The knowledge I acquire is not just information; it is the building block for the reality I hope to see in my country. 

I read these books to understand that every person has the ability to rewrite their own story. And with that belief, I know that even in the midst of limitations, it is still possible to write, to create, and to move forward. Every word I put on paper reminds me of this truth: real growth begins within us, not in the conditions that surround us. My journey is far from over, but I now walk it with the certainty that my mind is free, my purpose is defined, and my story is one that I am actively writing every single day. I hope that by sharing this, I can encourage other girls to find their own refuge in the power of words and to continue their journey of becoming, no matter how difficult the road may seem.
