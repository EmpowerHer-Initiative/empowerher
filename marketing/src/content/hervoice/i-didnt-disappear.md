There was a time when my life felt normal. Not perfect, but steady. I had things to do, places to go, and a reason to wake up and keep moving.
  
I didn’t think much about the future. I was just living it.  

Then everything changed.  

The things I used to do stopped. The routines I depended on slowly disappeared. And without warning, my life became very quiet.  

At first, I told myself it wouldn’t last. I thought it was just a pause, something temporary. But days turned into weeks, and nothing went back to how it was before.  

That’s when silence started to feel heavy.  

Not the kind of silence that is peaceful, but the kind that makes you think too much. The kind where you start questioning yourself, your place, your purpose. 
I didn’t know what to do with that feeling.  

Some days I felt stuck. Some days I felt like I was falling behind. And some days I didn’t feel much at all.  

I started noticing how silence changes you. It doesn’t come suddenly. It grows slowly, until even simple things feel difficult. Even your thoughts feel heavier than before.  

But even in that space, something inside me didn’t fully give up. 
 
It was small. Quiet. Not strong enough to call hope, but strong enough to keep me from stopping completely.
  
So I started doing small things again. I tried to bring structure back into my days, even if it was very little. I focused on simple routines, simple goals, simple steps that didn’t feel overwhelming.  

Some days I succeeded. Some days I didn’t. But I kept trying.  

And slowly, I began to understand something important. 
 
I didn’t disappear just because my life changed.  

I didn’t lose myself just because things stopped. 
 
I was still here.  

As Maya Angelou once said, “You may encounter many defeats, but you must not be defeated.” That thought became something I carried with me quietly, especially on the days I felt weakest.  

I realized that life doesn’t always fall apart loudly. Sometimes it just pauses. And in that pause,  you either lose yourself or slowly find yourself again.  

I am still in that process.  

Still learning how to move forward. Still rebuilding parts of myself I thought were gone. Still trying, even when it feels slow. 
 
But now I know something I didn’t know before: 
 
As Viktor E. Frankl said, “When we are no longer able to change a situation, we are  challenged to change ourselves.” And maybe that is what I am doing now.  

I am not sure what happened to me. 
I am what I choose to become after it.  
And I didn’t disappear.
